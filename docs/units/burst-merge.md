# Unit: burst_merge

- **Stage:** 8 (first fusion unit), host first; on the phone later through the microscope app
- **Path:** `core/burst_merge/` (C, like the other core units), host CLI `core/burst_merge/cli/`, evaluator `tools/fusion/`
- **Ported from:** "new". The streaming accumulator idea follows android-microscope `SuperResolution` (still-burst baseline) and android-multicam `MicroscopeEngine`; no code is copied.

## Why this shape

The plan's first fusion step: N frames of the same static scene, averaged, lower the noise by up to √N. It is the baseline every later fusion unit (`burst_sr`, `multicam_fusion`) must beat, and it needs nothing that does not exist yet: the microscope app already saves raw I420 bursts of up to 32 frames with timestamps. Alignment is **not** in this unit (the plan's input is "N aligned frames"; `align` 3.4 does not exist yet), so the precondition is a phone that does not move: on a stand, or lying still.

Measured before writing this contract (exploratory scratch script on the 2026-11-14 dark-scene bursts, to be reproduced by the evaluator below, not cited as evidence): camera 0 noise is temporally independent (residual correlation at every lag equals the −1/(N−1) bias of a 16-frame mean; averaging 4 and 8 frames matched √N within 1 %). Camera 2 through the bridge is **not**: residual correlation +0.25 at lag 1, about +0.1 at lag 2, no identical frames. Something on the HAL1 path (temporal noise reduction is the leading hypothesis, not established) makes consecutive frames share noise, so averaging gains less than √N there. The contract therefore judges the merge against the gain the frames' own correlation predicts, and reports the gain against √N separately.

## Contract

- **Input:** N ≥ 1 frames, each three planes (Y at w×h, U and V at w/2×h/2, 8-bit, any row stride ≥ the plane width), w and h even, all frames the same size, already aligned. Frames are added one at a time to an accumulator: memory does not grow with N (uint32 sums: 6 bytes per pixel, 72 MB at 12 MP, 9.3 MB at 1440x1080). N ≤ 16,777,216 (no overflow of a uint32 sum of 8-bit values).
- **Output (on finish):** the per-pixel mean as
  - `Y16`: luma in Q8.8 (uint16, value = round(256 · sum / N), ties up), so the averaged noise is not re-quantised to 8 bits (at σ = 1.8 and N = 16, 8-bit output would add 0.29 LSB of rounding noise to 0.45 LSB of averaged noise);
  - `I420`: 8-bit Y, U, V, each round(sum / N), ties up, for display and for units that take 8-bit input.
- **No spatial filtering.** Merging N identical frames returns that frame bit-exactly.
- **Errors (return codes, nothing written):** odd or zero size, null plane, stride below width, a frame of a different size, finish with N = 0, N above the cap.
- **Host CLI** `burst_merge_cli <burst_dir> <out_dir> --frames A-B`: reads `frame_NN.yuv` and `burst.json` written by the microscope app, merges the inclusive range, writes `merged_y16.pgm` (16-bit PGM), `merged.yuv` (I420) and `merge.json` (input dir, frame range, N, size). Exit codes 0/1/2/3 as in the other tools.
- **Evaluator** `tools/fusion/burst_merge_eval.py <burst_dir>`: runs the CLI on two **disjoint** frame ranges A and B of equal length N, and measures with NumPy, independently of the C code, on the centre half of the luma:
  - gain `G(N) = std(f_a − f_b) / std(A_N − B_N)` (f = single frames at the start of each range; the mean of each difference is removed first, so a slow exposure drift does not count as noise). No reference image is needed: A and B carry independent noise of the same size, so the scene cancels and the ratio is the noise reduction.
  - the predicted gain from the single frames' temporal variogram γ(k) = ½ var(f_{i+k} − f_i), averaged over all frame pairs at lag k: `G_pred = √(2 γ(N) / var(Ā − B̄))` with `var(Ā − B̄) = −Σ_{i<j} w_i w_j · 2γ(|i−j|)` for the merge weights w = +1/N on A and −1/N on B. It equals √N for independent noise. *Amended during the tests, before any device run:* the contract first used the mean-subtracted autocorrelation ρ(k) in `√N / √(1 + 2 Σ (1 − k/N) ρ(k))`; the evaluator's own test on synthetic AR(1) noise (ρ = 0.4, 32 frames) showed it 15 % off the true gain, because subtracting the temporal mean biases ρ low when the noise is correlated. The variogram form needs no mean, no noise level and no reference. `lag_correlation` (1 − γ(k)/γ(N)) is reported for information.
  - residual global shift between the mean images of A and B (phase correlation), reported as a motion check.
  Writes `results/<model>/burst_merge_<date>_<run>.json`; images stay with the burst outside the repo.

## Oracle

- **Host, exactness:** a double-precision mean computed in the test (not through the accumulator) on random frames with odd strides; `Y16` and `I420` must equal its rounding.
- **Host, noise:** a constant and a textured scene plus independent Gaussian noise (σ = 4 LSB, fixed seed, generated by the test with its own generator, clipped to 8 bits), 512×512. The scene cancels in the A−B difference, so the measured gain must match √N.
- **Device:** real Note8 bursts. Correctness of the merge is judged as agreement with `G_pred` from the frames' own statistics; usefulness is judged against √N, camera by camera.

## Thresholds (declared before the first run)

| Check | Threshold | Source of the number |
|---|---|---|
| `Y16` vs double mean | |error| ≤ 0.5/256 (exact rounding) | definition |
| `I420` vs double mean | |error| ≤ 0.5 LSB | definition |
| N identical frames | bit-exact copy of the frame | definition (no spatial filter) |
| synthetic gain, N = 4 and 16 | within 3 % of √N (2.0, 4.0) | std estimate on 262k pixels is ±0.2 %; 3 % leaves room for the 8-bit clip of the input |
| device: measured vs predicted gain | within 10 % of `G_pred(N)`, every camera | prediction uses lag correlations from 16 to 32 frames, noisier than the gain itself |
| device: camera 0 usefulness | G(8) ≥ 0.9·√8 = 2.55 on the 16-frame bursts; G(16) ≥ 3.6 on a 32-frame burst | √N for independent noise, which camera 0 showed in the exploratory check |
| device: camera 2 | recorded against √N, not judged | the correlation above; raising it is a bridge question (see below) |
| torch actually on | centre mean luma with torch ≥ 1.5 × without, **or** (amended after run 2) reported exposure without / with ≥ 1.5, same camera and scene; else "not shown" | an LED at 10 to 30 cm against an indoor room. Run 2 showed auto-exposure hides the LED in the luma of a lit room (luma ×1.10 while exposure fell ×8.8), so the exposure counts where the camera reports it; without either the result is "not shown", never "unlit" |
| motion check | integer shift between A and B means = (0, 0) with a phase-correlation peak ≥ 0.1, **and** (amended after run 2) the shift between the halves, measured per frame at sub-pixel level (one Lucas-Kanade step on the burst mean), explains ≤ 5 % of var(Ā − B̄); else "moved", not judged | a still phone; the peak floor rejects featureless scenes; 5 % of variance biases the gain by about 2.5 %. The integer check alone passed a 1 px drift whose half-means differed by 0.57 px (run 2) |

## Negative control

- 16 copies of one noisy frame must give gain 1.0 and **fail** the synthetic √N check: the gain cannot come from anything but independent frames.
- A merge with a spatial 3×3 box blur added (a test-only build flag on the fixture, not in the library) must fail the bit-exact identical-frames check: a false gain from smoothing is caught.
- The evaluator given overlapping ranges A and B must refuse (exit 2): overlapping noise inflates the gain.
- A synthetic burst whose B half is shifted by 3 pixels must be marked "moved".
- Odd size, N = 0, mismatched frame size, short stride: each must return its error code.
- A torch burst whose luma did not rise and whose exposure did not fall must be marked "not shown" by the evaluator (amended from "unlit", see the threshold).
- A synthetic burst drifting smoothly by 0.8 px, which the integer check passes, must be marked "moved" by the sub-pixel check; a still synthetic burst must not.

## Device runs

1. **Existing bursts, host only** (2026-11-14 dark scene, 16 frames, N = 8): camera 0 and camera 2 from run `175526-0da1`, camera 2 from `175008-fcca`. The dark scene (mean luma about 12 to 37) is below the shot-noise regime, so this is a first look, not the verdict.
2. **Lit scene, 32-frame bursts, flash LED as the light:** `run_live.py --cameras 0,2 --burst 32 --torch on` with the phone still over a textured surface (printed text or a coin, 10 to 30 cm away), then the same with `--torch off`. The torch makes the light the phone's own, so the run is repeatable without room light, and the two runs give two signal levels for the same scene. This run gives the verdict; its frame 0 also serves the pending NCC telephoto check in `microscope-live.md`.

   This needs a small amendment to the microscope app (`microscope-live.md`): a run-mode extra and a viewer button `torch`. Camera 0 gets `FLASH_MODE_TORCH` in its repeating request. Camera 2 goes through the bridge, which ignores request controls, so there the app calls `CameraManager.setTorchMode("0", true)` while camera 2 is open. Whether the camera service allows this while camera 2 holds the shared HAL is **unknown**; the report records whether the torch actually turned on (the call's result and the frame's mean luma with torch on vs off), and the run is only used as "lit" if the luma rises. If the torch is refused on camera 2, run 2 falls back to room light for camera 2 and says so.

## Not in this unit

Alignment, robust merging against moving parts (ghosting), sub-pixel super-resolution (`burst_sr`), cross-camera work, and merging on the phone (a JNI step after the host result). If camera 2's correlated noise is confirmed on the lit run, the follow-up is a bridge question: whether a HAL1 parameter turns the temporal filter off (`camera_params` reads them); this unit does not change the bridge.

With the first evaluator (integer motion check only) both camera 0 rows were "fail" (gain off its prediction); those files are kept as `…_cam0_integer-motion-check.json`. The cause was found by a per-frame sub-pixel shift fit (scratch analysis, then built into the evaluator): the camera 0 image drifts smoothly by about 1 px over the 2 s burst, the same direction in both runs. For camera 2 the request path (camera 2 reports a flash unit, so `on` chose `request`) may be ignored by the bridge; in a lit room this cannot be told, so it stays open.

OIS off cuts the drift about ten times and the slow correlation from 0.87 to 0.13: on camera 0 the stabiliser moves the image between burst frames of a still phone. For fusion on a stand, OIS must be off (`run_live.py --ois off`). Even then the run is "moved": in this bright, sharp scene the merged noise is so low that a 0.07 px shift between the halves is half of what is left. A 16-frame merge of camera 0 at this noise level therefore needs alignment to a few hundredths of a pixel, which is `align` (3.4) and then `burst_sr`, not this unit.
